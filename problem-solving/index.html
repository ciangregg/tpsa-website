About Us

We build public-interest tools where maths can help - and stop where it shouldn’t.

The Problem Solving Association CLG. is a not-for-profit social enterprise working to improve public services by bringing together technology, community knowledge, and social insight. Effective solutions to complex challenges need both technical innovation and legitimate engagement with the people these systems affect.

Our work has two complementary aims: applying mathematical and technological tools where they can help, and ensuring those same technologies don’t create new problems or deepen existing inequalities.

Ireland’s government spending, made legible

SPARÁN stands for

Spending Procurement Analysis, Reporting & Accountability Network

Public procurement generates vast transactional data, but it stays fragmented across departments and reporting systems. SPARÁN consolidates records from over 190 state entities covering €102.9 billion across 500,000+ transactions.

By building pipelines to extract, clean and standardise these records, we have worked together with Albert Dolan T.D.↗ to create a comprehensive view of state expenditure that is accessible to researchers, journalists, policymakers and citizens alike.

Provably fair electoral redistricting

COTHROM stands for

Constituency Optimisation Through Hamiltonian↗ Representation Of Mandates

We developed an algorithm for electoral redistricting based on a physics model for condensed-matter systems (the Potts model). Constitutional objectives are encoded into an “energy” description, then solved with advanced numerical techniques. Processing in minutes what takes a year by hand.

The end result will be quantifiable, consistent and auditable: gerrymander-resistant boundaries the public can verify.

Infrastructure planning under uncertainty

The decisions on where to place high voltage stations to serve communities for the next fifty years could save taxpayers millions if done correctly. However, traditional demographic forecasting struggles with the complexity and uncertainty of population dynamics. PRAXIS applies theoretical physics principles to this challenge, treating population patterns as a complex system exhibiting power laws and collective behavior.

We're building a collaborator network centred around Trinity College Dublin's School of Engineering including EirGrid, the Central Statistics Office, and the Irish Centre for High End Computing to develop stochastic forecasting models that provide probabilistic population projections with quantified confidence intervals. These forecasts will integrate directly with network optimisation algorithms, enabling infrastructure planning that accounts for demographic uncertainty while balancing economic efficiency, system resilience, and social equity. By bridging mathematical theory with practical policy implementation, we're ensuring Ireland's infrastructure investments will serve communities effectively for generations to come.

TPSAI: Ethical Compliance Workshop

Led by our Humanities Lead Jennifer Waters - an expert in Irish and EU law concerning artificial intelligence - TPSAI runs practical workshops for smaller businesses. The name says it plainly: TPSA + AI.
