Workshops & Hackathon 2026

Workshops

Once again we are running our annual python workshops for students. Workshops will run from 10:00 - 16:00 in the SNIAM computer room on the TCD Campus. This years workshop topics are as follows:

  • Monday 31/08 - Introduction to Python
  • Tuesday 01/09 - Graph-based computational techniques
  • Wednesday 02/09 - Introduction to Quantum Information and QuSpin
  • Thursday 03/09 - Topological Quantum Computation and Error Correction
  • Friday 04/09 - Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods

Hackathon

This year hackathon is presented by TPSA CLG. in conjunction with TPSA student association and TF Wind. Our topic this year is:

"Efficiency in Ireland's Electricity Grid"

Check below for more information on the workshops and hackathon, including the topics covered in each workshop and the details of the hackathon.

Monday 31/08 - Introduction to Python

Saoirse: Hi! I'm Saoirse and I'm covering the first workshop of the week with an introduction to python! This will be as open of a workshop as possible, covering coding practices and principles which are applicable in any coding language, whilst also teaching the syntax of specifically python. The main topics of the workshop will be variable assignment, operators, flow control (if statements, for and while loops, etc), python specific data structures (dictionaries, sets, lists and tuples), array usage and manipulation, functions, timing processes, object based programming, and using packages such as numpy, matplotlib, and pandas.

Tuesday 01/09 - Graph-based computational techniques

Cas: A workshop on computational techniques for analysing systems represented as graphs, using the graph Laplacian. Topics include spectral clustering, spatial autocorrelation statistics, geospatial network analysis, and numerical methods for solving diffusion-type PDEs on graphs. Some familiarity with linear algebra and NumPy is assumed.

Wednesday 02/09 - Introduction to Quantum Information and QuSpin

Conor: I will be covering some introductory topics in quantum information, including classical information theory, qubits, density operators, mixed vs pure states and entanglement. I will be exploring these topics through the QuSpin Python package. We will use this to create spin chains of different lengths, defining Hamiltonians which we will use to time-evolve the spin chains, seeing how different systems evolve over time.

Thursday 03/09 -Topological Quantum Computation and Error Correction

Matthew: The realisation of large-scale quantum computers faces serious experimental hurdles. This is because non-zero temperatures and magnetic fields interact with the quantum computer to destroy the information it stores and produce errors. In this workshop we will learn to understand an elegant approach to solving this problem which involves constructing qubits from a ‘topologically ordered phase of matter’.

While this sounds like a daunting task, it is perfectly approachable using standard quantum mechanics. We will take a lattice of spins and a Hamiltonian, investigate its energy spectrum and degeneracies, and identify many exotic consequences.

Friday 04/09 -Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods

Dave: From quantum field theory to magnetism, and everything in between, Monte Carlo simulations are a key item in a physicists toolbox, serving as an essential connection between theory and experiment in modern physics. This TPSA workshop aims to focus on exposing attendees to Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods, first through the underlying theory, and then through their computational implementation in simulations. The Potts model will be investigated in depth before venturing on to spin glass, showcasing the requirement for more advanced methods. Time-permitting, content on cluster algorithms and replica exchange may be included.

Monday 07/09 to Friday 09/09 - Hackathon

Our topic this year is "Efficiency in Ireland's Electricity Grid". You will be tasked with developing models and optimising decision making as they relate to Ireland's high voltage electrical infrastructure, with the goal of reducing the amount of wind energy wasted every year. You can utilise your knowledge of topics including electromagnetism, linear algebra and graph theory, as well as Python or other computational skills. Attending the TPSA workshops, particulary the workshop on 'Graph-Based Computational Techniques', can develop your abilities in these topics, but is not a requirement for participation.

The hackathon can be done in teams of up to 5 people. We will meet on the first day to give all the details of the problem. After that each team is free to work on the projects until the final presentation. Space in the new E3 Learning Foundry will be available for teams to work in, but you are free to work wherever and whenever you want. On Friday each group will present their progress on the questions. The problem will be broken down into steps that get increasingly more complex, so everyone can contribute, whether you are new to coding or have a lot of experience.

This is a great opportunity to develop your coding and problem solving skills, as well as work with friends and discuss topics with other students. Furthermore, previous hackathons have led to opportunities beyond the hackathon, such as being presented at a conference, a funded PhD and the COTHROM project you can read about below. This makes for a great career-boosting, as well as really fun, event!

Details about the location and the topic will be given before the hackathon to those who have signed up to the link above. If you have any questions about the hackathon, please contact us at tpsa@tcd.ie.
